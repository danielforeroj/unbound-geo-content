# Carousel

An Instagram carousel (also fine for LinkedIn). One idea, 7 to 10 slides, readable in under a minute, saved for later. The video is the entertainment. The carousel is the reference people keep.

## The grammar

- **Dark.** Near black background, off white text. Not pure white on pure black.
- **One accent color.** Used for the number, the framework name and the active element. Nothing else. No gradients, no second accent.
- **A named framework.** Give the fix a short name people can repeat ("The 4 Line Page"). The name appears on the framework slide and again on the example and closing slides.
- **One idea per slide, with a diagram.** Every content slide has a drawn thing (bars, arrows, before and after, a stack). A slide of only words is a failed slide.
- **Little text.** A headline of 8 words or fewer. At most 2 short lines under it. No paragraphs. If a slide needs a paragraph, it is two slides.
- **Counted means shown.** If the slide says "four lines", all four lines are on that slide, in full.
- **Numbers.** "N of M" with the bar or dots drawn to scale. A percentage only beside its count.
- **No real business names.** "A dental clinic with 312 reviews".
- **Same series look.** The grid, type, accent and slide numbers stay the same across posts so the account is recognizable.

## Slide by slide

Use this order. Merge or cut middle slides for a short carousel, but keep the first, the example, the payoff and the last.

| # | Role | What goes on it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover (hook) | The hook as a headline, 8 words or fewer, with the big number in the accent color. Series tag and "swipe". |
| 2 | The gap | A diagram of the main count. Two bars drawn to scale with "N of M (X%)" labels. One sentence at most. |
| 3 | The clue | What the engine cited. A flow diagram, question to answer to page. The report's citation count. |
| 4 | The framework | Name it. Show the whole framework in one diagram, all items in full. This is the slide people screenshot. |
| 5 | One part, deep | The one part of the framework that people get wrong, shown before and after. One idea only. |
| 6 | Example | A filled-in, concrete sample. A skeleton of the page, message or email, with placeholders in brackets for anything the report does not state. |
| 7 | Where to submit | The literal how. The product, the menu path, the button. Or who to email and what to say. |
| 8 | Payoff | What it earns, stated with the report's count. Not a promise. "The clinic that had one was cited in 7 of 10 answers." |
| 9 | Closing | A series hook ("Next post, the directories ChatGPT cited"), then the comment CTA with an outcome. Brand and slogan small, last. |

Nine slides is the default. Instagram allows up to 20 slides, but retention drops after 10.

## Slide design notes to return with the copy

Return a design note per slide so a designer or a template can build it.

- **Layout.** "Left aligned headline, diagram bottom two thirds."
- **Diagram.** "Two horizontal bars, 12 percent and 62 percent of the width, accent on the longer bar."
- **Emphasis.** The one element in accent color.

Do not include hex codes unless the user supplies the brand colors. Say "accent".

## Text rules on slides

- Headline is a claim or a question, never a label. "ChatGPT cited one page 7 of 10 times" beats "Citations".
- No colons in headlines. Use a period or a line break.
- US English. No em or en dashes.
- No filler words. Cut "very", "really", "actually", "just".
- Each slide's text must be true to the report line on the number check table.

## The example slide

The example slide is the proof the fix is doable. Rules.

- Show the real structure, filled with plausible content.
- Where the report does not state a fact (hours, prices, names), use a bracket placeholder: "[your Saturday hours]". Never invent a figure to make it look real.
- Keep it to the width of a phone screen. Short lines, a heading, four or fewer blocks.

## The payoff slide

Not a victory lap. It answers "so what do I get". State the observed fact from the report that shows why the fix matters, with its count, and no promise.

Good. "The clinic that had the page was cited in 7 of 10 answers."
Bad. "Do this and you will rank first."

## The closing slide

Two parts.

1. **Series hook.** Tease the next post with a specific, countable thing from the report. "Next. The 6 directory pages ChatGPT cited. This clinic is on 2."
2. **Comment CTA.** "Comment WORD and we'll send you X, so you can Y." See `cta.md`.

Brand and slogan go small at the bottom, last. "Unbound GEO. Get named. Then PROVE it."

## Before returning

- Count the slides. Nine is the default, ten at most.
- Every content slide has a diagram.
- Every number is in the check table with an R# line.
- Every counted list is complete on its slide.
- One accent. One framework name, used consistently.
- Closing slide has the series hook and the CTA.
