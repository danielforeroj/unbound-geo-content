# Call to action

The CTA promises an outcome and says what the viewer will have in hand afterward. It is the last thing in the video, and it comes before the brand line.

## The pattern

> Comment WORD and we'll send you X, so you can Y.

- **WORD.** One word, all caps, tied to the topic of this video, easy to type on a phone. SATURDAY, CITED, CHECKLIST. Not GEO or INFO. A unique word per video lets you know which video worked.
- **X.** A real thing that exists before you post. A template, an outline, a checklist, a sample email. Never "tips".
- **Y.** The outcome. What they can do with it, today. "publish it tonight", "see your own count in ten minutes".

Name the asset, name the outcome, say it once.

## Good examples

- "Comment SATURDAY and we'll send you the four line page outline, so you can publish it today."
- "Comment CITED and we'll send you the list of pages ChatGPT pointed to, so you can see which ones you're missing from."
- "Comment COUNT and we'll send you the question sheet we used, so you can run the same 10 tests on your own business."
- "Comment EMAIL and we'll send you the two sentence note to the local editor, so you can ask to be added to the next list."
- "Comment REPORT and we'll send you the free scan link, so you can see how many of your answers name you."

On X use a reply. "Reply SATURDAY and we'll DM you the four line page outline, so you can publish it today."

## Bad examples and why

- "Link in bio." No outcome, no word, no reason.
- "Follow for more." Asks for a favor and gives nothing.
- "Like and subscribe." Same.
- "Comment below if you want to know more." Vague. More of what? Nobody types.
- "DM us to level up your AI visibility." Vague, jargon, and no asset.
- "This is the last video I'll make on this, so grab it now." A fake deadline. Never close with a "last chance" or a last-email style slam.

## Rules

- The asset has to exist when the video goes live. If it does not, build it first or pick a different CTA.
- The CTA matches the video. A video about a Saturday page offers the Saturday page outline, not a general guide.
- One CTA. Not "comment, follow, and visit the site".
- Numbers in a CTA follow the same rule as everywhere else. "Four line page" needs R# in the check table. If the number is not in the report, write "short page".
- No promise of a ranking or a result. "so you can publish it today" is an action. "so you rank first" is a promise.
- No colon in the spoken CTA.
- The brand and the slogan come after the CTA, never before. "Unbound GEO. Get named. Then prove it."

## If the viewer wants Unbound to do the work

Some viewers will comment "can you just do it". The answer is one sentence.

> We do it for you, under one of our managed plans.

Use that exact line in replies, in the caption if the video is about a task that needs hands, and in the DM follow up. Do not name a price. Do not point them to a third party tool, an agency or a freelancer as the second option. If they do not want a managed plan, the second option is the free asset from the CTA, so they can do it themselves.

## Where the CTA goes

| Surface | Form |
|---|---|
| Video, last scene | Spoken CTA, then on screen text with the WORD in the accent color. |
| Caption | Same sentence, last line before hashtags. |
| Carousel closing slide | Same sentence under the series hook. |
| X thread, last tweet | "Reply WORD and we'll DM you X, so you can Y." |

Use the same WORD on all of them for one piece of content, so the count of replies tells you what worked.
