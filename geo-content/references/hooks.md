# Hooks

The first sentence decides whether anyone hears the second. It has two jobs at once.

1. **Open a curiosity gap.** Give a fact that raises a question the viewer can only answer by staying. The answer must exist later in the script.
2. **Carry an emotion.** Frustration, fear of loss, surprise, or the sting of "that's me". An emotion with no gap is a mood. A gap with no emotion is a quiz.

## The formula

> A specific fact from the report + a feeling + an unanswered question.

Three moves that work, in order of strength.

- **Count versus expectation.** "A 4.9 star clinic, named in 0 of 10 answers."
- **Contrast.** One clinic named 9 times, another 0 times, same question.
- **Second person stakes.** "Your Saturday hours are a picture." The viewer is the subject.

## Hard rules

- Never open with "In this video", "Today we", "Hey guys", "Did you know", or a greeting.
- No colon in the spoken hook. Use a period or a question mark.
- The hook uses a number from the report, written "N of M", or a plain fact from the report. No invented stat.
- No brand name in the hook. No real business name.
- Hook length is under 25 words. Read it out loud. If you run out of breath, cut.
- Pair the statement with a question or follow it with one short sentence. A question and an answer beat a statement.
- Never promise a ranking or a result in the hook.

## Eight good hooks

The figures below come from `examples/sample-report.md`. They are illustrations. In real use, swap in the figures from the report in front of you.

1. **"A clinic with a 4.9 star rating was named in 0 of 10 ChatGPT answers. Why?"**
   Why it works. The rating makes the zero painful. The viewer expects good reviews to win. The "why" is the gap.

2. **"ChatGPT sent 9 of 10 Saturday emergencies to the same clinic. Not this one."**
   Why it works. Contrast and a small sting. "Not this one" tells the viewer someone lost, and they want to know who and why.

3. **"Only 1 of the 14 pages ChatGPT cited belonged to the clinic. So who wrote the other pages?"**
   Why it works. Surprise. Most owners assume the AI reads their own site. The question points at a hidden set of sources.

4. **"Picture a patient with a cracked tooth on a Saturday, asking ChatGPT who to call. Your name was in 0 of 10 answers."**
   Why it works. A scene, then a loss. Use only when the report says "named in 0 of 10". The viewer sees the lost patient.

5. **"2,194 reviews against 312. Is that why ChatGPT keeps naming one clinic? Partly. Here's the part nobody checks."**
   Why it works. Numbers set up an obvious answer, then deny it. The gap is the "part nobody checks". Make sure the script delivers it.

6. **"Your Saturday hours are a picture in the footer. That could be costing you every emergency call."**
   Why it works. Second person, concrete, slightly embarrassing. Use only if the report states the hours are an image.

7. **"We asked ChatGPT the same question 10 times. One clinic showed up 9 times. Guess whose page it read."**
   Why it works. Rhythm, a count, and a small game. The viewer wants to find out what ChatGPT read. Deliver the cited page in the proof scene.

8. **"Four short lines on one page. That's the gap between a clinic ChatGPT names and one it skips."**
   Why it works. Small effort, big claim, specific count. Check the "gap" claim is stated in the report. If the report only shows a correlation, say "the page the other clinic had".

## Four bad hooks, and why

1. **"In this video we'll look at AI search visibility."**
   Why it fails. Banned opener, no gap, no emotion, no number. It announces a topic instead of making a promise.
   Rewrite. "A 4.9 star clinic, named in 0 of 10 ChatGPT answers. Why?"

2. **"Did you know AI is changing search forever?"**
   Why it fails. Everyone has heard it. No fact from the report, so no gap. "Forever" is a claim nobody can check.
   Rewrite. "ChatGPT named another clinic in 9 of 10 answers. Not yours."

3. **"Our clients' share of voice in ChatGPT is up. Here's how our platform did it."**
   Why it fails. Brand first, "share of voice" is banned, no count, and it is a boast. The viewer is not in the sentence.
   Rewrite. "One clinic was named in 31 of 50 answers. The other, 6 of 50. Same city."

4. **"Let me show you something. ChatGPT ranks clinics in a surprising order."**
   Why it fails. A colon-style pause in spoken form, a vague "something", and a ranking claim the report does not make. Surprise is promised, not delivered.
   Rewrite. "We asked ChatGPT for an emergency dentist 10 times. One clinic got named 9 times."

## Quick test

Before keeping a hook, answer four questions.

- Would a stranger understand it with the sound off, from on-screen text alone?
- Is there a number or a plain fact from the report in it?
- Can I name the feeling? (frustration, fear, surprise, sting)
- Does the script answer the gap by the 3/4 mark, not in the first 10 seconds?

If any answer is no, rewrite.
